.DISCUSSION...
As of 202 PM EST Friday...

KEY MESSAGE 1: Snow showers across southern Vermont will end this 
afternoon with a brief lull in precipitation for this evening. 
Clouds will remain as a reinforcing shortwave will shift south from 
Quebec later tonight into tomorrow. Wrap around moisture associated 
with the large upper level trough centered across our region will 
provide enough moisture to sustain upslope snow showers by tomorrow 
morning. As the shortwave dips into our area by tomorrow morning, 
blocked flow and orographic lift will lead to stratiform snow 
showers along our northwestern facing slopes in the northern 
Adirondacks and central and northern Greens. As the core of the 
shortwave passes through tomorrow early afternoon, our showery 
activity will turn more convective as marginal instability develops, 
allowing shower activity to survive the spine of the Greens into the 
eastern Greens, and survive the northwest slopes of the Adirondacks 
into the central northern Adirondacks. Snow showers will be 
strongest along the spine of the Greens with 3 to 5 inches of more 
powdery snow due to snow ratios around 10-15:1. Into the Adirondacks 
2 to 4 inches is expected, with the highest amounts in the High 
Peaks and northern Franklin County in New York between US-11 and 
Saranac Lake. Snow levels look to initially hover around 1200ft agl, 
but dynamical cooling could lead to snow level lowering down towards 
500ft, especially by tomorrow night in coordination with diurnal 
cooling. This could push some flakes into the eastern Champlain 
Valley where a few tenths of an inch of wet snow are possible. While 
snow amounts will be localized to the higher terrain, mountain 
passes, and untreated roads may become slick at times. Moreover, 
some localized power outages may be possible, but due to the drier 
character of the snow, impacts will be limited. 

As our snow turns more convective tomorrow afternoon into Saturday 
night, Lake Champlain streamers downstream of Lake Champlain look to 
develop as cold air aloft will be 10 degrees cooler than the current 
lake temperatures. Precipitation enhancement will be possible in the 
downstream regions of southern Chittenden and north and central 
Addison Counties. Precipitation types along this corridor will be 
mainly rain closest to the lake, with snow closer to the western 
slopes of the Greens, but dynamical cooling could push precipitation 
towards more snow closer to the lake at times. Accumulations look to 
be light as precipitable water will only be around 3 to 4 tenths of 
an inch. The position of any lake streamers are still somewhat 
uncertain, and will be dependent on the prevailing wind at the time, 
but it looks to be focused in an area between VT-22A and the
spine of the Greens.

Snow showers will lighten into Sunday, and taper off by Sunday 
evening, departing the highest summits of the Greens and NEK last. 
Solar heating, and gradual clearing will help to melt any snow in 
the lower elevations by Monday, with a warming and drying trend 
beginning early next week.

KEY MESSAGE 2: Confidence remains high that pervasive ridging will 
remain over the region next week keeping local weather dry and 
promoting a warming trend. High temperatures are favored in the 50s 
from Tuesday through Friday with lows ranging in the 20s to low 30s 
through mid week, climbing to around 30 to upper 30s by the end of 
the work week. Conditions for radiational cooling will be near 
ideal, so typically colder hollows will be running several degrees 
colder each morning.

.AVIATION /00Z SATURDAY THROUGH WEDNESDAY/...
Through 00Z Sunday...Mainly overcast skies continue, especially
north and west. With a few exceptions, like KRUT, most locations
have ceilings around 2500-4000 ft agl. Winds range from light
and variable to north or northwest around 4-9 knots. Ceilings
may lower some in the next few hours, but the main impacts will
come as a secondary trough to our north approaches the region
around 06z-14z, and then an upper low shifts south across the
area about 16z-04z Sunday. This will result in intervals of
showers across the area, with a few terminals cold enough for
snow to mix in or precipitation to be all snow. Some convective
elements are possible. Some graupel may be observed, as well as
heavier some briefly heavy rates that could result in sharper
drops in visibility. The greatest confidence of these impacts
are at KSLK after about 16z. Otherwise, kept visibility impacts
generally around 4-6SM.
